# Phase 18 — Visual Direction Lock (Change Set 119)

## Status
The flat red Direct Visual shown during engineering review is rejected as a visual direction. It remains useful only as proof that CPU deterministic rendering, SHA receipts, and integrity gates work.

## Approved direction to pursue
PUL7SAR visuals should read as premium sports editorial scenes rather than generic data cards:

- dramatic but credible sports-news composition;
- one dominant verified story subject or story object;
- controlled cinematic depth, stadium/venue atmosphere only when the story benefits from it;
- club/entity color used as a contextual accent, not a universal PUL7SAR background;
- concise editorial copy with a strong hierarchy rather than dense infographic copy;
- exact verified club marks and identity assets only;
- PUL7SAR hybrid-adaptive brand identity: metallic PUL7SAR body with dynamic pulse/7 accent;
- no legacy repository logo may become canonical merely because it exists in GitHub;
- generator, when used, owns atmosphere/scene pixels only and never invents readable PUL7SAR branding, exact scores, exact club marks, or verified identity.

## Reference-quality target
The accepted direction is closer to the previously reviewed football visual language: strong subject, club-linked accent color, tactical/lighting texture where relevant, restrained text, and a recognizable PUL7SAR signature. The goal is not to copy one fixed poster template. Story type controls the visual grammar.

## Immediate benchmark family
1. Transfer / signing — verified player subject + club context; no forced full pitch.
2. Result — neutral/respectful outcome treatment; no humiliation of loser.
3. Injury / statement — verified subject asset, restrained scene, no invented identity.
4. Tactics / data — deterministic geometry and data may bypass image generation.

## Publication blocker
The exact user-approved brand-master geometry still needs to be registered as deterministic master bytes/geometry. Until then, generated visual studies may test direction, but cannot establish a canonical public logo asset.
